Sports Attendance Trends · Australian Football League

Full House

Australians bought 8.5 million tickets to AFL games in 2026, more than in any season on record. This story follows those crowds: where they are, which clubs draw them, and what makes a stadium fill up.

Chapter 01

Bigger than ever

Crowds have grown for more than three decades. The pandemic wiped them out for two seasons, but fans came back in record numbers.

Thirty-seven seasons of crowds

In 1990 an average home-and-away game drew about 23,300 people. By 2026 that figure was 37,551, the highest in the 37 seasons shown.

The line is not a smooth climb. When Gold Coast (2011) and Greater Western Sydney (2012) joined, they added new games with small crowds in rugby league territory, so the average fell. In 2020 COVID-19 limits cut it to just 6,665.

Finals, shown dashed, have drawn between about 58,000 and 78,000 a game in normal seasons since 2013.

Hover over the chart to see any season's figures.

How the AFL compares

Each square is 50,000 tickets. In its latest season the AFL sold almost twice as many as the NRL, and more than the NRL, the A-League and the Big Bash put together.

Per game, the gap is smaller. The Big Bash plays only 44 matches, but averages 24,748 fans, about 13% more than the NRL's 21,860.

Chapter 02

A Victorian heartland

The AFL is a national league, but its crowds are not spread evenly across the nation. Three maps show where people actually turn up.

Melbourne outdraws the rest combined

Each circle is a city, sized by the total crowd at home games of the clubs based there. Melbourne's nine clubs drew 4.12 million people in 2025, more than every other city combined.

Perth and Adelaide each have two clubs and drew close to a million fans each. Sydney is bigger than both, yet its two clubs drew about half that. Brisbane's one club drew about a third.

Hover over a circle for exact figures.

Footy states and rugby league states

Size alone hides how much each state cares, so this map divides attendance by population. Victoria's clubs sold 635 tickets for every 1,000 Victorians, and South Australia comes next at 465.

New South Wales and Queensland, where rugby league is the main football code, sit below 90. Tasmania, shown in grey, has no club yet. Its own team joins the league in 2028.

The colour scale doubles at each step (a logarithmic scale), so big and small states can both be read. Grey states have no AFL club.

The MCG, and everywhere else

Each spike is a venue, and its height shows the total crowd there in 2025. The MCG alone hosted 38% of all AFL tickets. Add Marvel Stadium and Melbourne's two big grounds took 54%.

The AFL also takes games on the road, to Launceston, Hobart, Canberra, Darwin, Alice Springs, Bunbury, the Barossa, Ballarat and Adelaide's suburban Norwood Oval. Those 18 games drew about 10,600 fans each, just 2% of the year's crowds. They build fans in new places, not big gates.

Chapter 03

Club fortunes

Club crowds rise and fall from season to season. Who is drawing more fans, who is losing them, and how much does a big membership base help?

Who is rising, who is slipping

Every club gets one tile of the same size, placed roughly where it is based. That keeps small interstate clubs as visible as the Melbourne giants. Blue tiles grew their average home crowd, and red tiles shrank.

In 2025 the Western Bulldogs (+29%), Hawthorn and North Melbourne (both +15%) surged. Essendon (−21%) and Carlton (−16%) fell after their big 2024 seasons. Switch the seasons to see Melbourne drop 22% between 2023 and 2024.

Members fill the seats

Clubs with more paid-up members draw bigger home crowds, and the link is close. Collingwood had 110,628 members in 2024 and averaged almost 67,000 fans a game. GWS had 36,629 members and averaged about 12,000.

Each line runs from 2023 (hollow dot) to 2024 (filled dot). Twelve of the 18 clubs moved up and to the right, gaining members and crowds together. Melbourne and Richmond lost both.

Chapter 04

What fills the stands

The same club can draw 20,000 one week and 80,000 the next. Match-by-match data from the first 15 rounds of 2026 (121 games) shows three things that matter: when a game is played, who is playing, and where.

Prime time and public holidays

Night games averaged 44,600 fans, about a third more than day games (33,200). Thursday and Friday nights, built for television, are reliably big.

The biggest numbers come from public holidays. Easter Monday and King's Birthday drew 86,400 on average. Sunday twilight games are the hardest to fill.

Small numbers in each cell show how many games were played in that slot. Treat averages from one or two games with care.

Night games shift the whole spread

Averages can hide the full picture. This chart shows how crowd sizes are spread for each time slot: the taller the curve at a crowd size, the more games drew about that many people.

Day and twilight games bunch around 35,000. The night curve sits further right and has a second hump near 75,000. Those are the blockbuster night games at the MCG.

Click a legend entry to show one time slot on its own.

The Collingwood effect

Each row shows every 2026 game a club played, home or away. The middle line is the typical (median) crowd. Games involving Collingwood drew a median of 76,632, and the Magpies played in five of the six crowds above 80,000. When Collingwood comes to town, the stadium fills, whoever the home team is.

At the other end, Gold Coast and GWS drew under 20,000 in nine of their 13 games each. Their biggest crowds came when they were the away team.

How to read a box plot

The coloured box covers the middle half of a club's games: a quarter of games drew fewer people than the left edge, and a quarter drew more than the right edge. The white line inside is the median, the crowd at the exact middle game. The thin line runs from the smallest to the largest crowd, and each grey dot is one game.

What it adds up to

1

Record crowds, after a crash

Attendance has fully recovered from COVID-19. The 2026 home-and-away average is the highest in at least 37 seasons.

2

Geography still rules

Victoria, South Australia and Western Australia fill stadiums. The northern states, where rugby league dominates, remain the AFL's growth challenge.

3

Fixtures and fan bases drive gates

Night slots, public holidays, big memberships and Collingwood matter more to a crowd than a club's form in any one season.
